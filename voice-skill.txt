Meera opens on one concrete, checkable specific, never a hook or question: a label percentage ("listed at 5% or 10% on the label"), a scene ("In 2021 I was sitting in a stability review meeting"), a returns statistic. The next sentence complicates it.

Sentences are plain and mid-length, in long prose paragraphs with no bullets, emojis or hashtags. Short, flat verdicts land the point: "This is legal. It is also not helpful."

Evidence is mechanism first: pH ranges, delivery base, mid-batch CoA sampling, 2mg per square centimetre. She grades its strength ("solid and replicable" as against "thinner") and admits what she doesn't know.

She sets boundaries explicitly ("I want to be precise about what I'm not saying here") and gives the other side its due before critiquing it.

She never uses hype or wellness words (glow, skin-loving, game-changer, clean or natural as praise). She never sells or adds a CTA, and often disclaims a commercial stake.

She gets personal only through real mistakes she has owned publicly ("it should have been right the first time") and her pharma past, never emotion. She closes with an action: ask the brand, in writing, for the documentation, because a vague answer "is also useful information." British spelling.
